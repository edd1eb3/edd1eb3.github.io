The 2026 FIFA World Cup just started, the French Open just concluded, and the NY Knicks just clenched their first
NBA Championhsip in 53 years. Although I am an avid sports spectator, I am also a Computer Engineer, and I can't
help watching sports match without also noticing the technology employed nowadays and wondering about the designs
and architectures.

[IMAGE:0] [IMAGE:1]

Whether it's a Video Assistant Referee (VAR) that alerts match officials that a player was offsides in a FIFA
match, it's Electronic Line Calling (ELC)/ Hawk Eye in a tennis match, or it's the ability to challenge a call
in the NBA and MLB, technology is now pervasive in sports. Just like technology is pervasive in our daily lives,
in general, with the Internet of Things (IoT), with how our smart watches are synced to our mobile phones to send
us notifications, or even with how we can be listening to a song in the car and then seamlessly switch it over
to a Bluetooth speaker in our homes when we walk inside.

Many may read the word "pervasive" and think that it has a negative connotation, but I didn't say "invasive," I said
"pervasive" haha. The word, "pervasive," just means everywhere. "Pervasive Computing" is the proper term coined by
Mark Weiser in 1988, but I will refer to it as "ubicomp" which is short for it's other name, "ubiquitious computing,"
and sounds cooler =D

I won't delve too much into ubicomp because that's a topic for another blog and another day, but it's important to
recognize that Weiser envisioned VAR, ELC, etc back in 1988, officially publishing his paradigm in 1991. We're
talking about the late 80s, early 90s when the internet wasn't even accessible to the public yet until the mid-90s.
I remember when I was in Junior High School (in the mid-2000s) and SMART Boards were the "latest and greatest."
Weiser foresaw ALL this back in the 80s, decaaades before any of this technology even became widespread.

Ok, maybe he didn't specifically envision VAR, ELC, or whatever, but he definetly knew technology would seep into
every corner of our lives eventually.

But, let's re-focus our attention to technology in sports specifically again. Is technology ruining the spirit of
sports?

Some, who are more "traditionalists" or "purists," let's say, oppose technology in sports. They'll say,
"Remember when Frank Lampard should've had a goal in the 2010 World Cup vs Germany and there was no VAR back then?
If his goal was disallowed, then why should we allow a goal now upon further review and change the call? That's
inconsistent." <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gcAH4Qm5M84" target="_blank">
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gcAH4Qm5M84
</a>

And who remembers Zizou's penalty kick in the 2006 FIFA World Cup Finals that was actually allowed straight away?
<a href=""https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ilk8rVdcXRw"  target="_blank">
  "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ilk8rVdcXRw"

On the other hand, others would say, "Well, with this technology, there would be impartial calls that are objective.
We can't reverse incorrect calls in the past, but we can all at least agree that moving forward that this technology
is a fair system on which we can all reasonably agree."

And so it makes you wonder how do we balance sports technology that is fair and impartial, but also without ruining
the spirit of the game? I mean, imagine Babe Ruth or Joe DiMaggio challenging ABS in MLB? Just because they didn't have
this technology back then, should we be opposed to using it today? And, does it ruin the spirit of the game? How does
this inevitably pervasive technology get incorporated without being invasive instead and ruining the spirit of the game?
