the point of that long-winded update for what doesn't seem like much of a difference on the surface anyway
is the importance of the backbone of any website. you want a good foundation and make sure the design, architecture,
organization, or whatever is set up properly from the beginning to make your life easier as you go.

for example, the naive way of setting up the blog html page would be to use different tags </>
and put the text in paragraph tags, for example, [CODE]<p></p>[/CODE]
then put an image in an image tag, like [CODE]<img src="image1.jpg" alt="image1">[/CODE]
and so on ... here's a classic beginner's first html of what i mean:
[CODE]
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html>
<head>
    <title>Example Page</title>
</head>
<body>

    <h1>This is the FIRST header</h1>

    <p>This is text within the paragraph tags</p>

    <img src="image1.jpg" alt="Sample Image">

</body>

<body>

    <h1>This is the SECOND header</h1>

    <p>This is more text within the paragraph tags</p>

    <img src="image2.jpg" alt="Sample Image">

</body>

.
.
.
.
.
.
.

</html>
[/CODE]
the problem with this is that it is static and hard-coded.
i am not trying to make fun of beginners, but this is very common early on
(everyone learning html has made a variation of the above while first learning, too, btw)
but this does not have "depth," let's say, in its structure.

individuals like to organize things differently, but for myself, what i did was this:
- i have a directory called blog
  and another directory called home
  and can continue to add more pages as i go
- within each i have .css, .html, .ts
- i have a directory where i keep my .txt blog texts
- i have a directory where i keep my all images

so when i write my blog html, i am not hard-coding each, individual blog post, but instead i am writing one line of code
that REFERENCES the .txt files and then another line that references images, etc and use json also
in this way, i only write these lines of references one time; however, i can have use them for multiple references of text, images

i can get more depth about all this later, but just wanted to put an update and explain some of the foundation i've laid
for the website so far. i feel like sometimes spending more time doing these tedious things in the beginning just helps
you out in the long run
