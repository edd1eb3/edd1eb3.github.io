
this is not a super in depth explanation of ai, ml, llms, etc but is rather a quick
kinda breakdown to de-mystify some of the misconceptions of maching learning.

what even is intelligence? can intelligence be artificial? can machines "learn?"
large language models can "think" and they "think" in terms of language? what??
i'm not here to philosophize and answer those questions, but i will say that when you
really break it down, it's all basically just traditional statistics and mathematics.

the image below is a snippet from one of my ml courses. you don't necassarily need to
understand the mathematics, the context, or use-case to get my point that a lotta the
mystery of machine learning disappears when you can answer the question, "how did the
model get this solution?" and then realize that the model predicts outcomes in a very
similar way to how we would, except on a greater scale, and much more quickly.

[IMAGE:0]

think of it like this, if i were to tell you that there are 5 marbles in a bag, 4 being
red and 1 being blue, and you had to predict the color of a randomly chosen marble,
you'd say red, right? i'm oversimplifying, but you can see how these models calculate
more complicated probabilities than just 4/5 vs 1/5 similarly to us.

ml models do not really calculate much that we, humans, cannot calculate manually;
however, the main difference is the scale and especially the speed. if i asked you
what's 1 + 2, you can easily answer that quickly. if i asked you, though, what's the
sum of the range of integers from 1 to 10, it may take you a little bit longer, but you
can still do it in a reasonable time. but imagine if you had a calculator and didn't
have to do all that tedious addition? does knowing that 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 and that
1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10 really make a difference (conceptually)? not really. and imagine
how tedious it would be to write out the sum of 1 - 100 or even 1 - 1,000 in a notebook?
or, let's say, even if you had a calculator, how long would it take to punch in allll
those numbers?

now imagine that you can just punch in a few clicks on your keyboard and get an (almost)
instantaneous answer with a click of a button? a little something like

[CODE]

for (int i = 1; i <= 10; i++) { sum += i; }

[/CODE]

but at the end of the day it's just a summation. could we do these sums, theoretically?
yes, but it would take us forever. we understand the two images above are the same,
conceptually, and that both give the same result.

the german mathematician Ludolph van Ceulen apparently devoted 25 years of his life to
calculating the value of pi to 35 decimal places by 1596. you can read more about it:
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludolph_van_Ceulen)
but imagine his awe of seeing ai today, yet also the frustration he would surely
feel that 25 years of work can be achieved in fractions of a second! or how ancient
civilizations who only merely had abacuses (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abacus)
would react to seeing calculators. this would seem like magic to them, very mysterious.
but only because they cannot comprehend how the model comes to that solution so quickly.

and that is no different to how many regard technology today. but when you really
break it down, it's simply just traditional statistics and mathematics, which is not
as mysterious. maybe more tedious, but not as mysterious.
